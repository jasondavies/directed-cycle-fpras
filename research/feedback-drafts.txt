To: Antoine Amarilli
Subject: A proposed reliability reduction for directed-cycle probability

Dear Antoine,

I have prepared a short draft addressing your question about an FPRAS for
the probability that an independently edge-retained directed graph contains
a cycle. I would appreciate your view on whether this consequence is already
known and whether the reduction answers the question as intended.

For each edge e=(u,v), the witness event is that e is present and v reaches u
without e. These m events cover cyclic outcomes and have probabilities
p_e Rel_{G-e}(v,u). Reliability estimates also provide conditional samples
by sequential edge exposure. The classical inverse-multiplicity union
estimator then gives an approximation-preserving reduction to directed
two-terminal reliability.

The draft combines this with Feng, Fu, and Guo's Theorem 9, including a
finite-bit implementation of their Algorithm S. A companion Lean development
checks the probabilistic oracle reduction and resource bounds. The external
accuracy theorem, its larger-budget variance argument, and the machine-level
cost argument are not formalized. Whole-program subtype and parent-pointer
correspondences and the amplified program's fair-bit and output-size bounds
are checked. The work was developed with AI assistance.

The main reduction is in Sections 2–4; the computational model and precise
verification scope are in Sections 5–6. Any pointer to prior work or concern
about the argument would be very helpful.

Subject: Algorithm S discretization and a directed-cycle probability corollary

Dear Weiming, Yucheng, and Heng,

I have prepared a draft deriving an FPRAS for directed-cycle probability
from your two-terminal reliability result. Could you advise whether this
consequence is already known, and whether the use of Algorithm S in the
attached draft matches your Theorem 9?

The reduction uses one event per edge: the edge is present and its head
reaches its tail without that edge. It combines reliability-based conditional
sampling with the classical union estimator. To obtain a worst-case bit-model
statement, Section 5 discretizes Algorithm S at failure probability 1/8,
bounding both cumulative rounding error and rational operand lengths.

Section 6 also considers the variant retaining vertices unreachable from s.
Its restriction lemma preserves the exact output law at fixed M, with the
inherited arc order. It then uses your variance bound to justify computing M
from the larger, unreduced graph. Feedback on this transfer and on the
finite-bit adaptation would be particularly valuable.

There is a companion Lean development, but it assumes the accuracy of the
transcribed Algorithm S; it does not formalize your accuracy proof. The
larger-budget variance argument and machine-level bit-cost argument remain
written; subtype and parent-pointer output-law correspondences and composed
resource bounds are checked. The work and
draft were developed with AI assistance.
