Subject: A proposed reliability reduction for directed-cycle probability

Dear Antoine,

I have prepared a short draft addressing your question about an FPRAS for
the probability that an independently edge-retained directed graph contains
a cycle. I would appreciate your view on whether this consequence is already
known and whether the reduction answers the question as intended.

The draft uses vertex witnesses: splitting a vertex expresses the
event that it lies on a cycle as two-terminal reliability. A supplied
feedback vertex set gives a smaller cover. Recording and correcting the
nominal probabilities applies Jerrum–Valiant–Vazirani's classical rejection
correction to this weighted cover, for counting and conditional sampling. The draft
also gives an independent-set reduction for acyclicity-probability hardness.

The bit implementation uses Feng, Fu, and Guo's Algorithm S / Theorem 9.
The companion Lean development checks the vertex-witness rejection estimator,
conditional sampler, resource bounds, and exact complement-hardness gadget.
It also proves Algorithm S's accuracy bound for the concrete program. The work
was developed with AI assistance.

The main results and proofs are in Sections 1–5, with the verification
contract in Section 6 and correspondence details in the appendices. Was the
cycle-probability consequence already recognized after Feng–Fu–Guo, and is
this witness reduction useful enough to record separately? I would also
welcome prior references for the acyclicity-hardness consequence.

To: Weiming Feng, Yucheng Fu, and Heng Guo
Subject: Algorithm S discretization and a directed-cycle probability corollary

Dear Weiming, Yucheng, and Heng,

I have prepared a draft deriving an FPRAS for directed-cycle probability
from your two-terminal reliability result. Could you advise whether this
cycle consequence was already recognized following your result, whether the
witness reduction merits a separate short note, and whether the use of
Algorithm S in the attached draft matches your Theorem 9?

The reduction uses vertex-splitting reliability witnesses and a
corrected rejection estimator using the classical Jerrum–Valiant–Vazirani
method. Recording branch probabilities allows reliability accuracy Theta(1/m),
independent of the final relative error. The same identity gives sampling
conditioned on cyclicity. Section 4 and Appendix B discretize Algorithm S at
failure probability 1/8 and control rational operand lengths.

The correspondence appendix considers the Algorithm S transcription, retaining vertices
unreachable from s. Its subtype and parent-pointer correspondences are
checked. The formal variance proof works directly on the ambient graph;
feedback on the transcription and finite-bit adaptation would be valuable.

Lean proves the deferred-edge and snapshot-splitting identities, the concrete
conditional moments, and the martingale bound for the full Algorithm S program.
The counting and conditional-sampling theorems, including their finite-bit
implementations, therefore have no oracle-accuracy premise.
The work and draft were developed with AI assistance.
